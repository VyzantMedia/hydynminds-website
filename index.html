Questions people ask

Mostly the ones about working with someone on the other side of the world. Fair questions — here are straight answers.

The work itself
Do you come and film my event?

No. You film it, I edit it. That's the whole model, and it's why the turnaround is days instead of weeks and the cost is a fraction of hiring a local videographer.

A phone is genuinely enough. Most of what I work with is shot on an iPhone propped against a block at the back of the room.

What if my footage isn't very good?

Most of it isn't, at first. That's normal and it's fixable. Shaky handheld, uneven light, and phone audio that sounds thin are all things I deal with every week.

Three things make a real difference, and none of them cost anything: keep the phone still and let people move instead, shoot a few wide frames of the whole room plus a few closer details, and record a couple of minutes of clean audio with nobody talking so I have something to layer under the edit.

Send me what you have before your next event and I'll tell you specifically what to change. That's free.

How much footage do you need?

For a reel, five to ten minutes of clips is plenty. For a full event package, fifteen to twenty minutes gives me enough variety to build stories as well.

More isn't better. Ten focused minutes beats an hour of the same wide shot.

How do I actually send you the files?

Google Drive, Dropbox, or WeTransfer — whichever you already use. Upload straight from your phone, share the link, done. No file size limits to worry about and nothing for you to install.

If you'd rather not think about it, I'll set up a shared folder with your name on it and you just drop clips in after each event.

How many rounds of changes do I get?

Two rounds included, and I've rarely needed the second. Tell me plainly what's off — "the opening is too slow," "cut the shot of the woman in blue" — and I'll fix it.

If after two rounds it still isn't working, that's my problem, not yours. I'll keep going or refund you.

Can you match my existing brand look?

Yes. Send me your colors, fonts, and a few posts you like the look of. If you don't have a defined brand, I'll work from your Instagram and we'll settle on something consistent as we go.

Do you use AI to make this?

Not for anything you'd see. Your footage is your footage — no generated faces, no synthetic rooms, no AI voiceover pretending to be you.

I do use software tools for the unglamorous parts: cleaning up audio, stabilizing a shaky clip, generating caption text I then correct by hand. That's editing, not invention.

Timing & time zones
You're in India. Does the time difference slow things down?

Usually the opposite. I'm roughly 9.5 to 13.5 hours ahead of you depending on where you are. Footage you upload after an evening event is often edited and waiting in your inbox when you wake up.

The only place the gap genuinely matters is live conversation, which is why calls get booked a day ahead rather than same-hour.

When can we talk live?

Your mornings work best — that's my evening and I'm around. Book any slot that suits you and I'll be there. Occasionally your late evening works too.

How fast do you reply to email?

Same business day if you write during your morning. If you write late at night your time, you'll usually have a reply by the time you're up.

If something is genuinely urgent, text or WhatsApp me. I'll tell you the number once we're working together.

My event is in four days. Can you still help?

For a promotional video, probably yes if you can get me the details today. For post-event reels, timing isn't a problem at all — the clock only starts when you send footage.

Ask. The worst answer is no.

Money & paperwork
What does this cost?

Single-event work typically lands between $200 and $300 USD depending on how many pieces you need. Ongoing monthly arrangements work out lower per event.

I quote after we talk, because a single video and a monthly retainer building reels, stories, posts, and flyers across three events are genuinely different jobs. Tell me what's coming up and you'll have a number the same day — no discovery call sequence, no proposal deck.

How do I pay someone overseas? Is that complicated?

Not for you. You'll get a normal invoice and pay by card or bank transfer the same way you'd pay any US vendor. The cross-border part is handled on my end.

Everything is quoted and invoiced in USD, so there's no exchange rate for you to think about.

Do I have to pay before I see anything?

No. The first reel, story, or post is free — send footage, get an edit back, decide from there.

After that, the first paid project is half up front and half on delivery. Once we've worked together a couple of times, it's just invoiced on delivery.

Is there a contract? Am I locked in?

A one-page agreement covering scope, timing, payment, and who owns what. No minimum term, no auto-renewal, no cancellation fee. Monthly arrangements can be stopped with two weeks' notice.

If you want out, you're out. Chasing people who'd rather leave is a bad way to run a studio.

Who owns the finished videos?

You do, fully, once the invoice is paid. Use them anywhere — Instagram, your site, paid ads, a retreat brochure. No usage caps or expiry.

Any music I add is properly licensed for commercial use, so nothing gets flagged or muted later. Most of the time the best audio is your own room anyway.

What happens to my raw footage?

It stays in a private folder, and it's yours. I keep it for 90 days in case you want another cut, then delete it unless you ask me not to.

Nothing gets used as a portfolio piece, in an ad, or anywhere public without you agreeing in writing first.

What about the people in my circle — do they need to consent?

That's your call to make in the room, and I'd rather you make it carefully. Tell me who's opted out and I'll cut around them, blur them, or keep the framing wide enough that nobody's identifiable.

Faces mid-release are not content. If something feels too exposed to post, I'll flag it rather than hand it to you and let you find out later.

How do I know you'll actually deliver?

You don't, yet — which is exactly why the first reel, story, or post is free and costs you nothing but a file upload. You'll have real work in your hands before any money moves.

That's not a marketing offer. It's the only sensible way for a new studio to earn the benefit of the doubt.
